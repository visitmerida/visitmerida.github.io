Best Day Trips from Mérida

Mérida is the perfect base: world-famous Maya ruins, a yellow colonial city, flamingo lagoons, and Gulf beaches are all an easy day away. Here are the trips worth your time.

Chichén Itzá — the world wonder

Chichén Itzá is the Maya site the whole world knows, and it earns every bit of its fame. About two hours east of Mérida on the fast toll highway, this UNESCO World Heritage city was one of the greatest powers of the ancient Maya world, reaching its peak between 800 and 1200 AD.

The Temple of Kukulcán — El Castillo — rises 24 meters above the Great North Platform, its 365 steps marking the solar year; at the equinoxes, the afternoon sun paints a serpent of light slithering down the staircase. Walk to the Temple of the Warriors, the largest ball court in Mesoamerica, and the sacred cenote where offerings were thrown to the rain god. Climbing the structures is not allowed, so go early in the morning when the gates open for soft light, fewer crowds, and cooler air. Bring a hat, sunscreen, and plenty of water — and check current opening hours before you go. Pair it with a swim at Cenote Ik Kil, a short drive away, for the perfect ruins-plus-cenote day.

Uxmal & the Ruta Puuc — the connoisseur's ruins

If Chichén Itzá is the superstar, Uxmal is the connoisseur's favorite — many visitors leave saying it is the most beautiful Maya city they ever saw. About 80 kilometers south of Mérida, this Late Classic capital (around 800–1000 AD) is the crown jewel of the Puuc style, famous for its intricate stone mosaics of the rain god Chaac. The Pyramid of the Magician rises steeply above the entrance, wrapped in legend — it was said to have been built in a single night. Behind it lie the Nunnery Quadrangle with its carved façades and the Governor's Palace, a 100-meter-long masterpiece facing the evening sun.

Pair Uxmal with the smaller Puuc sites for a full day of stone mosaics: Kabah, where the Palace of the Masks is covered with hundreds of stone masks of Chaac, and Labná, home to the famously photographed corbel arch El Arco. Kabah is often sold on a combined ticket with Sayil, Labná, and Xlapak — ask at the gate. Come early or late in the day and wear good walking shoes.

Izamal — the Yellow City

About 70 km east of Mérida, Izamal is the "Yellow City" — nearly every building in the historic center is painted golden yellow, and the effect is magical. Its giant is Kinich Kakmó, dedicated to the sun god: one of the largest pyramids in all of Yucatán, free to visit and climb. From the top, the whole golden town spreads below you, with the colonial convent's atrium in the distance.

Do it in this order: pyramid first (morning light, fewer people), the Convento de San Antonio de Padua with its enormous atrium, a horse-drawn calesa ride through the yellow streets, lunch in the main square, and a cenote swim in the afternoon heat. ADO buses and colectivo vans run regularly from Mérida; driving takes about an hour.

Ek Balam & Valladolid — the climbable ruins

Ek Balam — "Black Jaguar" — is the site for travelers who want to climb. About 180 kilometers east of Mérida, near Valladolid, this Late Classic city guards one of the finest sculptures in the Maya world: the great stucco frieze on the Acropolis, where a jaguar's open mouth frames a carved king. The Acropolis rises 27 meters, and climbing its 106 steps rewards you with a 360-degree view over an ocean of jungle canopy (check the current climbing policy before you go — it can change).

Pair it with Valladolid, one of Yucatán's prettiest colonial towns: wander the pastel streets, visit the Convent of San Bernardino de Siena, and cool off in Cenote Zací, a swimming hole right in the middle of town. Order matters: town in the cooler morning, ruins when the light turns golden. Bring cash — smaller ticket windows are often cash-only.

Celestún — flamingos & biosphere

West of Mérida lies one of Yucatán's great wildlife spectacles: the pink flamingo colonies of the Ría Celestún Biosphere Reserve. Boat tours glide through mangrove tunnels and out to the shallow estuary where hundreds — sometimes thousands — of flamingos feed, turning the water pink. The ride also passes pelicans, herons, and often crocodiles resting on the banks.

Go in the morning when the light is best and the birds are most active; local boat cooperatives run the tours from the beach town of Celestún. After the tour, have a seafood lunch in town and walk the quiet beach. It's about 90 minutes west of Mérida by car or bus — bring binoculars if you have them, sunscreen, and a hat.

Progreso & Xcambó — beach plus ruins

The perfect two-for-one: Maya ruins in the morning, beach in the afternoon — all within an hour of Mérida. Start at Xcambó, a small coastal archaeological site near Progreso whose compact plaza and Temple of the Cross take under an hour to explore, often nearly to yourself.

Then head to Progreso for the rest of the day: walk the famous long pier, swim in the calm Gulf water, and lunch on fresh seafood at a beachfront restaurant. Frequent buses run from Mérida to Progreso in under an hour; Xcambó is easiest by car or taxi from Progreso.
